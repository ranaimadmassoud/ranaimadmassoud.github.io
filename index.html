Bio

I am a PhD candidate in computational physical chemistry at Michigan State University, specializing in molecular modeling, molecular dynamics simulations, enhanced sampling, and structure-based analysis of small molecule-protein interactions. My research integrates computational chemistry with toxicological and biological questions to understand how chemicals interact with molecular targets and contribute to adverse biological outcomes.

My research has increasingly focused on computational toxicology and environmental health, particularly the molecular mechanisms underlying endocrine disruption by environmental contaminants. A major focus of my work is per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), where I investigate their interactions with nuclear receptors across aquatic and mammalian models using molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, free-energy calculations, and structure-based analyses. These studies aim to connect chemical structure and molecular interactions with receptor modulation and potential downstream toxicological outcomes.

I am particularly interested in developing mechanistic approaches for studying environmental chemicals and complex chemical exposures. My work includes investigating nuclear-receptor interactions, chemical mixtures, species-specific molecular responses, and structure-activity relationships, with an emphasis on generating mechanistic information that can contribute to New Approach Methodologies (NAMs), adverse outcome pathway (AOP) development, and environmental risk assessment.

In addition to environmental toxicology, I have contributed to structure-based drug discovery projects involving Mycobacterium tuberculosis targets and GPCRs. These projects have provided experience in molecular docking, pharmacophore modeling, fragment-based approaches, molecular dynamics simulations, and computational analysis of ligand-protein interactions.

My broader research goal is to bridge molecular-level computational approaches with experimental and systems-level toxicology. I am particularly interested in integrating computational modeling with emerging toxicological data to improve mechanistic understanding of chemical hazards, support development of NAMs, and contribute to predictive and regulatory-relevant approaches for chemical safety assessment.

Selected Research Projects

  • PFAS–Nuclear Receptor Interactions in Aquatic Invertebrates: Molecular mechanisms of PFAS-mediated modulation of the ecdysone receptor and implications for adverse biological outcomes.
  • PFAS Mixtures and Nuclear Receptor Interactions in Fathead Minnow: Computational investigation of PFAS mixture interactions with estrogen and androgen receptors.
  • PFAS and Biotransformation Products in Rainbow Trout: Molecular modeling of PFAS interactions with estrogen receptors and investigation of potential estrogenic activity.
  • Food and Textile Dyes and Estrogenic Activity: Computational and experimental investigation of chemical interactions associated with estrogenic activity.
  • Structure-Based Discovery of MmpL3 Inhibitors in Mycobacterium tuberculosis: Molecular docking, molecular dynamics, and computational characterization of ligand–target interactions.
  • Computational Discovery of GPR68 Inhibitors: Structure-based modeling and computational screening of potential therapeutic compounds.
